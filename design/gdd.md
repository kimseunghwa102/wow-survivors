## 2. First Minutes & How to Play

| Time | Player experience |
|---|---|
| **0–5 seconds after control** | You spawn in the Sanctuary. A glowing gate stands ahead with a HOW TO PLAY board beside it. |
| **5–10 seconds** | You walk through the gate and pick one of three starting skills. The swarm comes; your skill fires on its own. |
| **10–60 seconds** | You move to dodge and collect gems. The first level-up freezes the swarm and offers three cards; you pick one and see it fire. |
| **1–3 minutes** | Your build takes shape: weapons, passives, boons. At Lv 10 the first boss, Gravemaw, rises; beating it opens a vault. |
| **3–10 minutes** | A skill reaches max; with its partner passive the next vault ascends it. Arena events (Blood Moon, Gold Rush) change the run. The Blood Ring sign warns that hunters fight there. |
| **Natural stopping point** | You die or walk out; gold banks to your wallet. The board shows today's three quests, your streak and the next boss. |

**Player-facing How to Play**

- Walk through the gate, survive the swarm
- Collect gems, level up, pick upgrades
- In the Blood Ring, hunters fight hunters

## 8. Audience & Comparables

**Primary player + arrival context**

For players who already enjoy survivors-like games (Vampire Survivors, Brotato), arriving alone from Discover or a shared clip, looking for a five-minute run that keeps counting toward something big.

**How the first group arrives**

From Discover and short clips on X and TikTok, because one boss fight makes a 20-second video. Every run is in one shared arena, so even a few players online overlap.

**Deliberately not for**

Players who want a slow story or building — this is fast arcade combat.

### Comparables

| | Comparable A — Decentraland: Antrom RPG | Comparable B — outside Decentraland: Vampire Survivors |

| What we observed works | Hunting monsters, clearing missions and running raids together with other people; choosing weapons and upgrading them through missions; rich graphics that show what a Decentraland scene can look like | One-stick play, auto-firing skills, three-card level-ups and evolutions make the first minute obvious and every run different |
| What does not fit our audience or context—and why | `[agent-decided]` Missions and raids take planning and a group; a solo phone player with five minutes needs a faster start | Fully solo — nothing changes when another person is nearby; runs are 30 minutes, long for mobile |
| What we will do differently | `[agent-decided]` Keep the instant solo run, and bring the group raid feeling into the same arena with a drop-in Hunt Party | One shared arena with visible hunters, an opt-in PvP block, and progress that banks every 5 minutes |

| 4 — Playable release, including testing and deployment | Party board on the Hall of Legends; mobile playtest at 20 hunters; release in our scene |

**What keeps the experience changing after launch**

- Without building a new level, variation comes from random arena events and build rolls; the daily quests change every day.
- If an update is skipped, the weekly board reset and prizes still create variation.
- If progression creates a power gap, the Blood Ring is opt-in and the swarm scales to each hunter's level; a new player contributes to a party within 5 minutes.
- One player behaviour that would change what we build next: if parties form but split before the first boss, the party gets its own early boss.

**Not building in v1**

1. The hold'em room and paid gold in the World — standing non-goal: no gambling in the funded game, because it fails the Content Policy.
2. The Pale Sovereign co-op raid on parcel -91,71 — first to come back in v2.
3. New boss tiers beyond Lv 1111.

**Top risk + fallback.**

Top risk: the game runs on each player's client, so a modified client could fake results. Fallback: the server already caps gold, kills and level per second; v1 adds party-wide caps and a review queue for flagged runs.
